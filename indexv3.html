What rbassist does for you

Stop prepping. Start practicing.

Six things you used to do by hand on a Tuesday night, now handled before you sit down. Every step is a dry-run first — you approve what ships back to Rekordbox.

01 / Listen Auto

It listens through your library.

Every track gets analysed locally for energy, mood, texture, vocals, and groove — the qualities you actually mix on, beyond BPM and key.

02 / Grids Auto

It fixes grids that drift.

Beatgrids re-aligned to the downbeat across variable-tempo edits, live takes, and old rips. Phrase markers land on the bar, not three frames late.

03 / Cues Auto

It builds your hot & memory cues.

Intros, drops, breakdowns, outros — beat-aligned, phrase-aware, colour-coded the way you set them last time. Across 3,000 tracks before Friday.

04 / Tags Learns you

It learns the tags you actually use.

Not someone else’s taxonomy. It studies how you tag — warmup, peak-rolling, weirdo closer — and applies them to the rest.

05 / Crates Auto

It expands crates without mush.

Seed a 30-track folder, get 120 tracks that actually belong — with diversity controls so it doesn’t just hand you twelve copies of the same idea.

06 / Repair Dry-run

It cleans the library chaos.

Broken paths, stale references, duplicate detection, missing artwork, malformed metadata — surfaced as a diff you review before anything writes back.

Vibe search

Search your library the way you describe a vibe to a friend.

Say what you want — “late-night dubby rollers, warm bass, sparse vocals” — and rbassist pulls it from your own folder. The AI is the ears: it heard every track in your library so you don’t have to remember them all. BPM-aware, key-aware, duplicate-aware. No streaming catalogue, no upload.

Crate Expander

Grow a playlist without flattening the vibe.

Drop in a Rekordbox playlist, pick a mode, dial in similarity vs. diversity. rbassist expands the crate using the seed tracks you trust — keeping BPM and key in pocket, suppressing same-artist clusters, exporting back to Rekordbox XML.

  • 01
    Three modes, real differencesTight pulls in close neighbours. Balanced is the default. Adventurous loosens BPM/key and bumps diversity — for digging, not for the main set.
  • 02
    Role lanes that map to a setTell it whether you’re building warm-up, peak-time, tools, or closers — and it ranks by what fits that slot, not just “similar.”
  • 03
    Camelot or soft key matchingStrict adjacency for harmonic sets, or soft mode when you’d rather not lose a great track over a semitone.
  • 04
    Anti-repetition that actually worksSuppresses same-artist runs, same-title-stem dupes, and version clusters so the crate doesn’t devolve into one producer’s discography.
  • 05
    Export to Rekordbox XMLNo destructive writes. You import the new playlist into Rekordbox yourself, when you’re happy with it.

Auto-cues

Set hot & memory cues on the whole crate, not one track at a time.

rbassist listens for drops, breaks, and mix-points, then writes hot and memory cues on beat-aligned positions using your own template. Skip tracks that already have cues, or force overwrite. Write them straight back to Rekordbox.

  • 01
    Templates, not hardcoded slotsIntro / mix-in / drop / core / mix-out — the dj_workflow profile is config-driven so you can shape it to how you mix.
  • 02
    Beat-aligned, not just “close”Cues snap to the beatgrid (BeatNet CRNN with a librosa fallback). No off-grid hot cues messing up your loops.
  • 03
    Batch the whole libraryProcess thousands of tracks with skip-existing on. Overwrite is one toggle, never a default.
  • 04
    Rekordbox writes are backed upEvery cue write produces a timestamped sidecar so you can roll back if a template change misfires.
AI tagging

Tags that learn how you tag, not someone else’s genre tree.

Train rbassist on the tags you actually use — “rolling,” “dubby,” “warm bass,” whatever you’ve built — and it suggests tags on the rest of your library. Confidence threshold is yours. AI tags live in their own namespace, so they never overwrite the ones you wrote by hand.

  • 01
    Your tagging style, not theirsActive learning picks the most uncertain tracks for you to label, so a small effort goes a long way.
  • 02
    User tags vs. AI tags, kept separateTwo namespaces. AI suggestions never silently overwrite what you already tagged. Promote them when you’re ready.
  • 03
    Review-first, alwaysSuggestions sit in a queue. Accept, reject, edit, or batch-approve above your confidence threshold.

Library repair

Clean up the library without wrecking the library.

Years of downloads leave broken paths, half-tagged files, duplicate versions, and tracks that wandered out of your music root. rbassist audits all of it, shows you a plain-language report, and changes nothing until you approve.

  • 01
    Dry-run first, alwaysEvery repair produces a report before it touches a tag or a path. Inspect the JSON/Markdown, then apply.
  • 02
    Duplicates aren’t deleted — they’re hiddenSuspected dupes live in an overlay you can review. rbassist never moves a file to the recycle bin on its own.
  • 03
    Rekordbox is a secondary truthrbassist reconciles XML and DB state against your real files. It never overwrites Rekordbox without an explicit confirm.

The AI, explained like a DJ would use it

Different ears for different parts of the prep.

Under the hood, rbassist is a small studio of AI listeners. Each one is good at a different job — one hears how tracks groove together, one understands the words you use to describe a vibe. Two are doing the heavy lifting today; two more are in the lab, and you can A/B them yourself before they ever touch your main flow.

Your library, your machine

Your library stays yours.

Nothing uploads. Nothing trains on your collection. Every destructive action is reviewable, reversible, and runs against a backup. DJs protect their libraries by inspecting before they apply — rbassist is built the same way.

  • 01
    Local metadata and model sidecarsEmbeddings, indexes, and analysis live next to your files, on your disk.
  • 02
    Dry-run-first repair workflowsEvery destructive operation produces a report before it touches a tag or path.
  • 03
    Backup-first metadata mutationTag and Rekordbox writes are backed up to a timestamped sidecar before they apply.
  • 04
    Rekordbox treated as a secondary truth sourcerbassist reconciles, never overwrites Rekordbox without explicit operator approval.
  • 05
    Duplicate suppression overlay, not deletionSuspected duplicates are hidden and reviewable — never auto-deleted from disk.
  • 06
    AI tags stay in their own namespaceUser tags and AI tags never collide. Promote AI tags into user space only when you accept them.

rbassist · vs · Rekordbox

Rekordbox is where you play. rbassist is where you get ready.

rbassist isn’t trying to be a DJ app. It sits one step before Rekordbox — the place where the unglamorous work happens — and hands you back a library that’s actually ready to mix.

Rekordbox Loads & plays the set.
rbassist Prepares the library.
One is a performance tool; the other is the assistant who got everything ready for the performance.
Rekordbox Reads tags you type.
rbassist Learns how you tag.
Your vocabulary — rolling, weirdo closer, peak-rolling — applied to the rest of the library.
Rekordbox Filters by BPM & key.
rbassist Searches by vibe.
BPM and key still apply — on top of mood, texture, energy, and how a track actually grooves.
Rekordbox One track at a time.
rbassist The whole crate, in batch.
Grids, cues, tags, expansion across thousands of tracks — ship the diff, then open Rekordbox.
Rekordbox Your set; your responsibility.
rbassist The prep; reviewable.
rbassist never decides what you play. It just makes sure the library is in shape when you do.
Rekordbox Cloud-tethered.
rbassist Local, always.
Your files, your machine, your GPU. Nothing leaves the laptop unless you put it there.

rbassist reads and writes Rekordbox XML. It doesn’t replace Rekordbox · it stands beside it.
